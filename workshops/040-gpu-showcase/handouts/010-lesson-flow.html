What an H100 Unlocks: Throughput + Real Research

NAIRR's big GPUs aren't just for demos — they're a research instrument. Here's a 20‑minute proof.

The big idea

On a full GPU we (1) serve a model at thousands of tokens/sec, then (2) run an actual reproducible benchmark study — how accurately open LLMs answer cybersecurity exam questions, and whether a bigger model helps. It's a known benchmark, not original work, but it's exactly the workflow real evaluation research uses — and it's impossible on a laptop.

⚠️

Cost — shelve or delete when done

A full GPU (g5.xl / H100) is the most SU‑expensive flavor. Run the demo, then shelve (frees your quota, stops SU charges, keeps the disk) or delete the instance. Don't leave it idle.

H100 Research Showcase
Throughput demo → MMLU benchmark (1.7B vs 8B) → synthetic‑data bonus

What happens

1 · Throughput

vLLM serves a 128‑prompt batch at once; we report aggregate tokens/sec — the GPU saturated.

2 · Research benchmark

Evaluate accuracy on MMLU computer_security; compare a 1.7B vs 8B model. Output: an accuracy table + chart, and a clear finding (bigger → better).

3 · Bonus

Generate synthetic labeled data (phishing vs. legit emails) — the pattern behind LLM‑built training sets.

🎓

The takeaway for researchers

Evaluate at scale, run realistic model sizes, and produce reproducible results in minutes. Extend it: more models/subjects, accuracy‑vs‑quantization (ties to 020-quantization), or train a classifier on the synthetic data.

🖥️

Where it runs

A full GPU instance (g5.xl / H100, or g4.xl / L40S). Launch & start Jupyter as in Workshop 010.
